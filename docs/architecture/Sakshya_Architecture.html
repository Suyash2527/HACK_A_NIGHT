2 · Evidence upload workflow

From a police officer's upload to a sealed, signed, audited and chain-committed record.
1
Authenticate & authoriseCognito JWT verified. Policy engine checks role, case assignment and the role→document-type matrix. RDS row-level security repeats the check in the database. A refusal is logged as DENIED.
2
Validate≤ 4 MiB; pdf / docx / txt / png / jpg; server recomputes everything — nothing from the client is trusted.
3
Fingerprint plaintextSHA-256 of the file; 4 KiB chunk Merkle tree → chunkRoot + chunk leaf list.
4
Encrypt in partsRandom 256-bit key (DEK). File split into 64 KiB parts; each part AES-256-GCM encrypted (AAD = docId|index|count). Part address = SHA-256 of the stored bytes.
5
Wrap the key (post-quantum)ML-KEM-768 encapsulation → HKDF-SHA256 → key-encryption key → AES-GCM wraps the DEK. Only the wrapped key is stored.
6
SignUploader's ML-DSA-65 key signs the canonical JSON {docId, caseId, type, version, sha256, chunkRoot, partRoot, timestamp}.
7
StoreCiphertext parts → S3 (by hash). Metadata, wrapped key, signature → RDS (append-only). Plaintext never touches disk or DB.
8
RecordAudit entry (with legal basis) hash-chained; case manifest version +1; record registered on the chain registry.
Receipt shown to the userplaintext SHA-256 · AES-256-GCM ✔ · ML-KEM-768 ✔ · ML-DSA-65 signature ✔ · audit entry ID · status UNANCHORED → PENDING_ANCHOR → VERIFIED

What is stored where

ItemWhere

Corrections never overwrite: a new version is appended with parentId, and the registry enforces version > parent.version.

3 · Verification & tamper detection

Runs on every view, on demand, and in integrity scans. Six independent checks; green only when all pass.
① Parts present & unalteredFetch each part; SHA-256(bytes) must equal its address. An edited part is named without decrypting: "part 2 altered".
② Authenticated decryptionUnwrap key (ML-KEM-768), AES-256-GCM decrypt each part. Swapped, reordered or cross-document parts fail.
③ Plaintext SHA-256Recompute and compare with the signed original.
④ Chunk Merkle rootDiff 4 KiB chunk hashes → exact chunk + byte range, e.g. chunk 2, bytes 8192–12287.
⑤ ML-DSA-65 signatureVerified off-chain by our backend verifier; verdict + evidence hash attested on chain.
⑥ Batch proof & on-chain rootDocument leaf ∈ batch root; batch root equals the root stored on the chain.

Insider-attack scenario (demo centrepiece)

AttackInsider with key access decrypts, flips one plaintext byte (offset 9000), re-encrypts validly, overwrites the stored object.
▶
Why decryption passesGCM is valid for the new bytes, so "can it be opened?" says yes. This is why decryption alone is not enough.
▶
DetectionSHA-256 ≠ signed hash · chunk root ≠ original · part address ≠ stored. Chunk 2, bytes 8192–12287. Last accessors listed (context, not proof of guilt).
▶
RecoveryS3 Object Lock kept the original version. Authorised restore appends a new signed version; the damaged one stays on record in red.
StatusMeaningColour
VERIFIEDall six checks passgreen
TAMPEREDa content / hash / part check failed; ranges reportedred
PENDING_ANCHORcontent checks pass; batch not yet confirmed on chainamber
UNANCHORED / ERRORnot committed yet / could not verify — never shown as greengrey / red

"Assemble case folder" (court / prosecutor)

1
Policy check; advocates only get documents granted to them.
2
Fetch every part by hash; verify address.
3
Decrypt, rebuild each document, verify hash / chunks / signature.
4
Recompute the manifest root; compare with the on-chain root.
5
Live progress "38/42 parts verified" → ZIP bundle (files + manifest.json + hash certificate) only if everything is VERIFIED. Audited as FOLDER_ASSEMBLED.
Splitting per document (not the whole folder as one blob) keeps access granular and lets new evidence be added later.

5 · Court request workflow & complainant progress

Advocates obtain documents only through a judge's order; everything — who asked, who decided, who looked — is recorded with its legal basis.
1 · Advocate filesdocuments wanted, legal basis + citation, reason → REQUEST_FILED
2 · Judge reviewssees the request, case and documents
3 · DecisionGrant (all or subset, optional expiry) with order reference + note, or Reject → REQUEST_GRANTED / REJECTED
4 · AccessView + watermarked download (advocate, order ref, hash, time); every access audited with COURT_ORDER
5 · ExpiryAccess ends at expiry; the full trail remains

Every audit entry records

who (pseudonymous on chain) · what action · which document / case · when · legal basis {code, citation, order ref} · previous hash → entry hash (chain). The legal basis is inside the hash, so the reason cannot be edited later.

Legal basis codes

INVESTIGATION · FORENSIC_ASSIGNMENT · PROSECUTION_ASSIGNMENT · JUDICIAL_AUTHORITY · COURT_ORDER · STATUTORY_DISCLOSURE · VICTIM_INFORMATION · SYSTEM_INTEGRITY
Citations are entered by the judge / advocate and stored verbatim. The system records the basis; it does not interpret law. Statute section numbers should be confirmed with legal counsel before being quoted.

Complainant progress view

FIR registeredInvestigationForensic analysisCharge sheetTrialJudgment
Milestones are added by the investigating officer or the court (audited). The complainant sees dates and public-safe notes only — no documents, hashes of evidence, or other parties.

8 · Trust boundaries & honest status

What the system proves — and what it does not

ClaimBasis
A record existed unchanged at time Thash committed on chain at T
Content was altered (and where)hash / chunk / part mismatch
Who accessed it, and whyhash-chained audit with legal basis
The ML-DSA signature is validOur backend verifier checked it; the chain stores its attestation. The blockchain does not verify post-quantum signatures. Anyone can re-verify from the stored signature and key hashes.
Recent accessors are guiltyNot claimed — context only
Post-quantum scope: ML-KEM-768 protects document-key wrapping and ML-DSA-65 signs commitments. The chain's own consensus and transaction signatures are not quantum-safe. Trust dependencies: the backend verifier and the protection of signing / decryption keys (KMS).
